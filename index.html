Inglewood Just Voted to Seize a City Block for Its Olympic Transit Hub

Inglewood just voted to seize an entire city block on Market Street, and it is one of the biggest signs yet that this neighborhood is changing fast.

The City Council voted 4-0 to force the sale of eight properties at Market Street and Florence Avenue to build a brand new transit hub connecting the Metro K Line directly to SoFi Stadium, Kia Forum, and Intuit Dome. Think bus bays, EV charging, a parking structure, and a pedestrian bridge over Florence Avenue. The city has set aside $125 million to make it happen, with completion targeted before the 2028 Olympics.

Here is the part that matters for investors. The demolition is happening at the south end of Market Street. 720 N. Market sits north of it, meaning this property gets the benefit of a transit hub being built down the block without any of the disruption. When a Metro connected hub opens on your street ahead of the FIFA World Cup, Super Bowl LXI, and the 2028 Olympics, rents do not stay where they are.

Inglewood's Historic Market Street Is Getting an $8.5M Facelift

Inglewood's historic Market Street is getting a major makeover. The City Council voted unanimously to distribute $8.5 million in state grant money to businesses along the corridor running south from Florence Avenue, with grants of up to $250,000 going to nearly 36 restaurants and retailers for full interior and exterior renovations. The city is targeting completion before the 2028 Olympic Games, and the work is already underway right outside the doors of 720 N. Market Street.

For years, Market Street sat largely dormant while the rest of Inglewood boomed around SoFi Stadium and Intuit Dome. That is now changing fast. Mayor James T. Butts has set his sights on turning the corridor into a destination rivaling Old Town Pasadena, and $8.5 million in public funding is what is backing that vision.

For apartment owners, this is the part that matters most. When a city invests millions into beautifying the exact street your building sits on, your property value rises without you spending a dollar. Stronger foot traffic means stronger tenant demand, lower vacancy, and more leverage to raise rents at renewal.

Inglewood is also set to host the FIFA World Cup this summer, Super Bowl LXI in 2027, and the 2028 Olympics. Each event brings global attention and outside investors competing for a limited pool of quality properties, which means valuations will only keep climbing. Buying now means buying before this market fully reprices.

Long Beach’s Density Bonus Ordinance Is Opening Doors for Multifamily Development

Long Beach encourages multifamily housing development through its Enhanced Density Bonus Ordinance, which provides incentives for projects that include affordable housing. Depending on location, eligible developments can receive density bonuses of up to 70% in most areas and up to 100% near major transit corridors and stations.

One active example is Armory Arts Collective, currently under construction in Downtown Long Beach. The project will convert the historic Armory building into 64 affordable senior apartment homes through adaptive reuse while adding new residential construction alongside it.

Investment Thesis June 2026 3 min read

How “Space Beach” Is Fueling Long-Term Apartment Demand in Long Beach

Long Beach’s aerospace industry has rebounded significantly since Boeing closed its final Long Beach manufacturing facility, earning the city the nickname “Space Beach.” The city is now home to dozens of aerospace startups backed by venture capital, advanced manufacturing, and launch technology. California State University, Long Beach (CSULB) is a central part of this shift. Its aerospace engineering program ranks No. 3 in the nation among non-doctoral programs, and through its Space Beach Initiative, CSULB is building direct partnerships between the university, industry, and government. Over the next decade, the school expects to produce approximately 15,000 engineers and technical professionals.

For Long Beach’s apartment market, aerospace expansion creates sustained housing demand. As companies grow and CSULB graduates stay in the region, the renter pool grows alongside it. The Port of Long Beach, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing add further diversity to the employment base.

Atlantic Avenue’s Redevelopment Push Is Putting North Long Beach on Investors’ Radar

North Long Beach continues to attract both public and private investment along the Atlantic Avenue corridor. The City of Long Beach has placed 11 city-owned parcels between 55th and 60th Streets on the market as part of its “Develop, Build, and Activate” initiative. The sites are intended for a mix of housing, retail, restaurants, office space, and community uses, building on nearby projects including the Artesia Great Boulevard improvements, Houghton Park upgrades, Rhythm by KB Home, and a planned neighborhood grocery store.

Private developers are also active in the area. A proposed seven-story, 82-unit apartment building at 402 Atlantic Avenue would convert a long-vacant commercial property into multifamily housing with rooftop terraces, a pool deck, and ground-floor retail.

Market Research July 2026 8 min read

Inglewood Multifamily: Vacancy, Cap Rates, and Buyer Behavior

Inglewood’s multifamily market is more stable than earlier corridor-specific data suggested. The previous vacancy figure came from a narrower area between SoFi Stadium and LAX. The updated CoStar report covers the broader City of Inglewood, drawing on 119 sales records across 115 properties with sale dates after July 6, 2024.

Current occupancy sits at approximately 96.11% compared to 95.93% one year ago, implying a vacancy rate of approximately 3.89%. The overall picture is stability. For sellers, high occupancy supports the case that renter demand remains steady. For buyers, stable occupancy does not automatically mean strong rent growth, so underwriting should still be based on actual rent rolls, collections, expenses, and realistic lease-up expectations.

The average transaction cap rate across the market is approximately 5.9%. Properties are selling at an average of 5% below asking price after roughly 3.7 months on market. Local private capital accounts for approximately 84% of sales volume, and approximately 60% of buyers are local.

One property-level example: 3835 W 104th St (Inglewood Palms), a 28-unit building offered at $6.375 million ($227,700 per unit). Its vacancy of approximately 7.1% runs above the 5.8% submarket average for comparable properties, and average asking rent of $1,574 is below the $1,701 submarket average. A buyer could evaluate whether improved leasing and management could close that gap over time.

A regulatory note: Inglewood permits short-term rentals but requires a city permit under Ordinance 22-15. Investors should not assume event-driven rental income is available without confirming a property qualifies.

Source: CoStar Sale Comps Analytics, Inglewood Multifamily Market, 2026.
