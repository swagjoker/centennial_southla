Inglewood Just Used Eminent Domain to Build a Transit Hub — Here’s What It Means for Investors

Inglewood just voted to seize an entire city block on Market Street, and it is one of the biggest signs yet that this neighborhood is changing fast. The City Council voted 4-0 to force the sale of eight properties at Market Street and Florence Avenue to build a brand new transit hub connecting the Metro K Line directly to SoFi Stadium, Kia Forum, and Intuit Dome. The city has set aside $125 million to make it happen, with completion targeted before the 2028 Olympics. Here is the part that matters for investors. The demolition is happening at the south end of Market Street. 720 N. Market sits north of it, meaning this property gets the benefit of a transit hub being built down the block without any of the disruption. When a Metro-connected hub opens on your street ahead of the FIFA World Cup, Super Bowl LXI, and the 2028 Olympics, rents do not stay where they are. 720 N. Market Street is a fully renovated 16-unit building with $750,000 already invested in capital improvements, including solar panels, a new roof, copper repipe, and fully remodeled units. Strong in-place cash flow, a transit hub being built at the end of the block, and a global event calendar driving demand for the next three years. This is what buying before the market fully reprices looks like.

Market Street Is Getting an $8.5M Facelift Before the Olympics

Inglewood’s historic Market Street is getting a major makeover. The City Council voted unanimously to distribute $8.5 million in state grant money to businesses along the corridor running south from Florence Avenue, with grants of up to $250,000 going to nearly 36 restaurants and retailers for full interior and exterior renovations. The city is targeting completion before the 2028 Olympic Games, and the work is already underway right outside the doors of 720 N. Market Street. For years, Market Street sat largely dormant while the rest of Inglewood boomed around SoFi Stadium and Intuit Dome. That is now changing fast. Mayor James T. Butts has set his sights on turning the corridor into a destination rivaling Old Town Pasadena, and $8.5 million in public funding is what is backing that vision. For apartment owners, this is the part that matters most: when a city invests millions into beautifying the exact street your building sits on, your property value rises without you spending a dollar. Stronger foot traffic means stronger tenant demand, lower vacancy, and more leverage to raise rents at renewal. Inglewood is also set to host the FIFA World Cup this summer, Super Bowl LXI in 2027, and the 2028 Olympics. Each event brings global attention and outside investors competing for a limited pool of quality properties. Buying now means buying before this market fully reprices.

Long Beach’s Density Bonus Ordinance Is Opening Doors for Multifamily Development

Long Beach continues to encourage multifamily housing development through policies such as the Enhanced Density Bonus Ordinance, which provides developers with incentives to include affordable housing in new projects. Depending on a project’s location, eligible developments can receive density bonuses of up to 70% in many areas and up to 100% near major transit corridors and stations. The City’s goal is to increase housing production while placing new development near jobs, transit, and neighborhood amenities.

One example of this continued investment is Armory Arts Collective, a project currently under construction in Downtown Long Beach. The development will transform the historic Armory building into 64 affordable senior apartment homes through adaptive reuse while adding new residential construction. Although the project serves a specific housing need, it reflects the City’s broader commitment to expanding multifamily housing and revitalizing underutilized properties.

For apartment owners and investors, these initiatives demonstrate that Long Beach remains committed to increasing housing opportunities and encouraging redevelopment. Properties with redevelopment potential, particularly those located near transit or major corridors, may become more attractive to buyers seeking long-term value. As the market continues to evolve, understanding how local policies influence development can help owners make more informed investment and disposition decisions.

How “Space Beach” Is Fueling Long-Term Apartment Demand in Long Beach

Long Beach’s aerospace industry has undergone a remarkable transformation, earning the nickname “Space Beach” as the city has become one of the nation’s fastest-growing hubs for commercial space innovation. Just 11 years after Boeing closed its final Long Beach manufacturing facility, the city is now home to dozens of aerospace startups supported by venture capital, advanced manufacturing, and launch technology. A key driver of this resurgence is California State University, Long Beach (CSULB), whose aerospace engineering program ranks No. 3 in the nation among non-doctoral programs. Through its Space Beach Initiative, CSULB is strengthening partnerships between industry, government, and education while preparing the next generation of engineers. Over the next decade, the university expects to produce approximately 15,000 engineers and technical professionals to help meet the growing workforce demand.

For Long Beach’s apartment market, continued aerospace growth represents more than new jobs. It creates long-term housing demand. As aerospace companies expand and CSULB graduates remain in the region, demand for quality rental housing is expected to increase. A growing base of high-skilled, high-paying jobs helps strengthen occupancy, supports rent growth, and reinforces Long Beach’s position as a desirable market for multifamily investment. Combined with continued investment from the Port of Long Beach, healthcare, and advanced manufacturing, the city’s increasingly diversified economy provides a strong foundation for apartment demand.

Long Beach’s transformation into “Space Beach” demonstrates how economic development and workforce growth continue to shape the local multifamily market. Understanding how emerging industries influence housing demand is critical for apartment owners and investors evaluating future opportunities.

Atlantic Avenue’s Redevelopment Push Is Putting North Long Beach on Investors’ Radar

North Long Beach continues to gain momentum as both public and private investment reshape the Atlantic Avenue corridor. The City of Long Beach has officially placed 11 city-owned parcels between 55th and 60th Streets on the market as part of its “Develop, Build, and Activate” initiative. The sites are intended for a mix of housing, neighborhood retail, restaurants, office space, and other community-serving uses, building on nearby investments such as the million Artesia Great Boulevard project, Houghton Park improvements, Rhythm by KB Home, and a planned neighborhood grocery store.

At the same time, private developers are continuing to invest in the area. A recently proposed seven-story, 82-unit apartment community at 402 Atlantic Avenue would transform a long-vacant commercial property into modern multifamily housing with amenities including rooftop terraces, outdoor gathering spaces, a resort-style pool deck, and ground-floor retail. Together, these projects demonstrate growing confidence in Long Beach’s long-term apartment market and illustrate how public improvements can encourage additional private investment.

For apartment owners, these developments are worth watching. Continued investment in neighborhood amenities, new housing, and commercial activity can strengthen buyer confidence, improve the area’s appeal to renters, and support long-term property values. At Centennial Advisers, we closely monitor the trends shaping Long Beach’s apartment market. If you’re interested in learning how redevelopment, neighborhood investment, or changing market conditions may affect your property’s value, contact our team to discuss your investment strategy.
